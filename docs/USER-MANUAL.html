1 Before you start

The Site Visit Scheduler is where O&M work is planned: the calendar of technician visits, the workload view, and the O&M ticket board. It reads and writes live Airtable data, so anything you change here is immediately real.

You do not need an Airtable account

Earlier versions asked each person to paste an Airtable token into the page. That is gone. You sign in with an email address and a password, and the server holds the only Airtable credential. Nothing about your Airtable access matters any more — only the role an administrator gave you.

One account, two applications

The scheduler and the Delivery Note app share the same accounts and the same four roles. If you can sign in to one, the same email and password work on the other.

You do sign in separately at each address, because a browser will not carry a session between two different websites. That is a browser rule, not a setting anyone can change.

What you need

  • The web address, from your administrator
  • Your work email address
  • A password — either one you set from an invitation, or a temporary one an administrator gave you

Any current browser works. There is nothing to install.

2 Signing in

Your first time

  1. Open the address. The sign-in panel appears over the page.
  2. Enter your email address and password, then choose Sign in.
  3. If you were given a temporary password, you are asked to set your own straight away. You cannot skip this, and the temporary one stops working once you do.
  4. The scheduler loads on the Calendar screen. Your name and role appear at the top right.

If you forget your password

Choose Forgot password? on the sign-in panel and enter your email. If that address has an account, a reset link is sent to it.

Why the message never confirms your address

The screen says the same thing whether or not the address has an account, and a wrong password gives the same message as an unknown email. That is deliberate: it stops an outsider using the sign-in page to work out who has accounts.

Lockout, sessions and timeouts

RuleSettingWhat it means for you
Failed sign-ins 5 attempts The account locks for 15 minutes. Waiting clears it; no administrator needed.
Session length 8 hours You are signed out after eight hours, whatever you are doing.
Idle timeout 30 minutes Leave the tab untouched for half an hour and you must sign in again.
Two-factor code Off Email and password only. An administrator can switch this on for Admins.

Use Log out when you finish on a shared machine. It ends the session on the server, not just in your browser, so the tab cannot be reopened into your account.

3 The three screens

The tabs at the top left switch between three views of the same O&M data. Which of them you can use depends on your role — see section 5.

ScreenWhat it is forWhat you can do
Calendar Scheduled site visits by Month, Week or Day. Open a visit to see its detail. Use + Schedule Site Visit to add one. Filter by technician, status or owner category, or search by job card, site or client.
Workload How visits are distributed across technicians and time. Spot who is overloaded and where there is space before you book more work.
O&M Tickets The job card board — maintenance and callout work. Open a job card, read its history, add notes, and update status where your role allows it.

The green dot beside Refresh shows the connection is live, how many visits are loaded, and the time of the last update. The view also refreshes on its own while the tab is open.

4 How access works

Your role controls three separate things. All three apply at once, and each can only narrow what the previous one allowed.

Screens — which parts of the app exist for you

A role that has no site visits does not get an empty calendar; it gets a short explanation instead. Nothing is shown greyed-out or teasing.

Records — which rows you can see

ScopeYou seeMatched on
All Records Every visit and job card in the O&M base. —
Assigned Only Only work assigned to you. Site visits match Technician Email; job cards match Assigned To (Email), against your sign-in address.
If assigned work is missing

A visit only reaches a technician on Assigned Only if the record's Technician Email exactly matches their sign-in address. A blank or mistyped email means the visit is invisible to them — the commonest cause of "my calendar is empty".

Fields — which columns you can read and change

Even inside a record you can open, individual fields can be hidden or read-only. Financial columns on a job card are the main example. Section 7 lists exactly which.

Hidden means absent

A field your role cannot read is removed by the server before the page receives it. It is not merely hidden by the screen — it never arrives, so it cannot appear in a search, an export, or by inspecting the page.

Admin / Director

Sees and does everything: all visits and job cards, all financial fields, deletion, export, and the Admin panel for accounts, access levels and the audit log. This role should be held by as few people as the business can manage.

Operations / Project Manager

The everyday planning role. Sees every visit and job card, creates and edits them, and exports. Two limits: cannot delete anything, and cannot manage users or change access levels. Ops can view documents up to Confidential but export only up to Internal — so the most sensitive material can be read on screen and not carried out of the system.

Technician / Field

Sees the calendar and tickets, but only work assigned to them. They can create and edit their own visits and update job card progress. They cannot export at all, cannot delete, and cannot see any financial field on a job card — no budget, cost, project value or invoice.

On a job card a technician may change only four things: Status, Progress %, Site Status and Resource Status. Every other field is read-only for them, so field updates cannot disturb commercial data.

Warehouse / Stores

Has no site visits, so this application shows them a short panel reading "No access to the scheduler" and nothing else. This is correct, not a fault. Warehouse staff work in the Delivery Note app, where they have full access to their own job.

If a Warehouse user needs the calendar

Do not work around it by sharing another person's sign-in. Ask an administrator to change the role, or add site visit access to the Warehouse access level. Both are recorded in the audit log; a shared password is not.

Reading document sensitivity

Documents are labelled Public, Internal, Confidential or Restricted. A role has a ceiling for viewing and a second, lower ceiling for exporting. An unlabelled document is treated as Restricted — the most protected setting, not the least — so forgetting to classify something never exposes it.

8 Administrator guide

Administrators get an Admin button beside Log out. It opens a panel with three tabs. Nobody else sees the button, and nobody else can reach what it does.

These changes affect both applications

The scheduler and the Delivery Note app share one set of accounts. Creating, suspending or re-roling someone here changes them in both — there is no scheduler-only user list. Do it once; do not create a second account for the same person.

8.1 Users

The list shows everyone, filterable by status. Selecting a person opens their detail.

Inviting someone

  1. Choose Invite a user.
  2. Enter their name, work email, job title and mobile.
  3. Choose the access level — this is the decision that matters. See section 5.
  4. Set the record scope: All Records, or Assigned Only for a technician.
  5. Choose Send invitation. They receive a link and set their own password; you never see it.
If email is not configured

Without a mail key set, the invitation is not sent — the link is written to the server log instead. Send it to the person yourself, through a channel you trust. Do not post it in a group chat: whoever opens it first sets the password.

Disable, do not delete

Deleting a user record breaks the audit trail: entries that name them lose the person they refer to. Disabled stops all access just as firmly and keeps the history readable. Changing status takes effect at their next request — combine it with Revoke sessions to end access immediately.

The two buttons on a user

  • Force password reset — they keep their access but must set a new password at their next sign-in. Use after a suspected shoulder-surf, a shared password, or when someone has been told their password over the phone.
  • Revoke sessions — signs them out everywhere immediately, on every device. Use for a lost or stolen phone, or the moment someone leaves. The count on the button is how many sessions are currently open.

Per-user overrides

Below the role you can narrow a single person without creating a new access level: restrict them to named clients or sites, set an approval limit in Rands, or grant the restricted-documents flag. Overrides only ever tighten what the role allows — they can never widen it.

8.2 Access levels

This tab edits what a whole role may do. A change here affects every person holding that role, in both applications, immediately.

Change one thing at a time

Save, then sign in as somebody with that role and confirm the effect before changing anything else. An access level edited in several places at once is very hard to unpick when something stops working.

Anything not explicitly listed is denied. Removing a line does not loosen access — it removes it. Adding a role or an area you have not tested is the one change worth making outside working hours.

8.3 Audit log

Every security-significant action is recorded permanently: sign-ins and failures, lockouts, password changes, permission edits, account changes, and every view of restricted material. Search it by person or action, and Export CSV for a record.

Denied attempts are logged as well as allowed ones. A run of denials from one account is worth asking about — usually it is someone confused about their role, occasionally it is not.

8.4 A monthly ten minutes

  • Filter users to Active and confirm every one still works here.
  • Check how many people hold Admin / Director. It should be a very short list.
  • Skim the audit log for repeated failures or unfamiliar activity.
  • Confirm anyone who left is Disabled with sessions revoked.

9 Rules that cannot be overridden

These hold regardless of role, and regardless of what the screen appears to offer. They are enforced on the server, where the browser cannot reach them.

RuleWhat it means in practice
The browser holds no Airtable key One credential lives on the server. Your browser holds only a session cookie it cannot read, so nothing extractable from the page reaches Airtable.
Anything unlisted is denied Access is granted by naming it. An area not listed in an access level is refused — no permission is ever inherited by accident.
The screen is not the control Hiding a button is a courtesy. The same request sent by hand is refused just the same, because every request is re-checked against your role.
Passwords are never recoverable Stored one-way only. Not visible to Admins, not in exports, not in email, not in the audit log. Forgotten passwords are reset.
Out of scope reads as missing A record outside your scope returns "not found", not "not allowed", so the refusal itself cannot be used to confirm a record exists.
Both outcomes are logged Refused attempts are recorded alongside successful ones, with who, what and when.

10 Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
"No access to the scheduler" Your role has no site visits — normally Warehouse / Stores. Correct for that role. If your job has changed, ask an administrator to change your access level.
Calendar is empty but colleagues see visits You are on Assigned Only and nothing matches your address. Ask for Technician Email on your visits to be checked against your sign-in address.
Costs and budgets missing on a job card Working as intended for Technician and Warehouse. Nothing. Ask an Ops/PM or Admin if you need a figure.
A field will not accept a change It is read-only for your role. Technicians may write four job card fields. Ask an Ops/PM to make the change.
"Invalid email or password" Either one is wrong; the message never says which. Retry carefully, then use Forgot password?. Stop before five attempts.
"Too many failed attempts" Five failures; locked for 15 minutes. Wait, then use the reset link. No administrator is needed.
Signed out unexpectedly 30 minutes idle, or the 8-hour limit, or an administrator revoked sessions. Sign in again. If it repeats within minutes, tell an administrator.
Signed in here but not on the Delivery app Normal — sessions do not cross between two addresses. Sign in again at that address with the same details.
"Could not load the schedule" The data did not load; the message says why. Use Refresh. If it persists, send the exact wording to an administrator.
Never do this

Do not share a password or sign in as someone else, even briefly and even to help. Every action is recorded against the account that performed it, so borrowed access attributes your work to another person and theirs to you — and it hands them whatever your role can reach. If someone needs access, that is a two-minute change for an administrator.
