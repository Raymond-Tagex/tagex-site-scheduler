1 Getting oriented

This manual covers using the scheduler. For who can see what, and for creating and managing accounts, see the companion O&M Scheduler Handbook.

The top bar

+ Schedule Site VisitTop rightOpens a blank visit form, with no date pre-filled.
RefreshTop rightReloads immediately. Rarely needed — see below.

It updates itself

The scheduler reloads every 60 seconds while the tab is visible, so a colleague's booking appears without you doing anything. It pauses while the tab is in the background and catches up when you return. Use Refresh only when you want to be certain right now — for instance while someone is on the phone with you.

Everything is live

There is no draft mode and no save-at-the-end. The moment you save a visit it is real, visible to everyone, and part of the record. There is no undo — a mistake is corrected by editing or cancelling, both of which leave a trail.

All times are SAST

Every time you see or type is South African Standard Time (UTC+2). There is no daylight saving to allow for, and the app does not shift times if you travel.

2 The calendar

Three views

ViewBest forBooking behaviour
MonthSeeing the shape of the month and spotting empty weeks.Clicking an empty day opens a new visit on that date; you set the time.
WeekThe normal planning view — the week's load across every technician.Clicking an empty slot opens a new visit at that date and hour.
DayTight days, and checking one technician's run in order.Same as Week — the hour you click becomes the start time.

Move with ‹ and › either side of Today. They step by whichever view you are in — a month, a week, or a day. Today always returns to now.

Reading a visit block

Each block shows the time span, the job card reference, the client and site, and the technician. Its colour is the visit type (section 5), so a week's mix of work reads at a glance without opening anything.

Filtering

Four controls sit above the calendar. They combine, and they apply to whichever view is showing.

  • All technicians — one person's diary. The fastest way to answer "what is Maurice doing on Thursday?"
  • All statuses — hide cancelled and completed work to see only what is still live.
  • All owner categories — narrow to a class of client.
  • Search — free text across job card, site and client.
A filter looks exactly like an empty calendar

If work seems to have vanished, check these four controls before anything else. A technician filter left on from yesterday is the usual explanation.

3 Booking a visit

  1. Click the day or hour you want in the calendar — the date and time arrive pre-filled. Or use + Schedule Site Visit for a blank form.
  2. Pick the Job Card first. The subject fills itself in, and the client's owner category appears underneath so you can confirm you have the right one.
  3. Check Start and End. Both are SAST.
  4. Choose the Technician, then Visit Type, Priority and Status.
  5. Add any Additional Attendees, and put access notes, gate codes or equipment needs in Notes.
  6. Save Visit. It appears on the calendar at once, and for everyone else within a minute.

The form, field by field

Meeting Subject
Fills from the job card. Overwrite it only when the job card title would not tell a technician what this particular visit is for.
Job Card
Ties the visit to real work. Everything downstream — workload, reporting, the ticket history — hangs off this link.Set this first
Start · End
SAST. A visit with no end is treated as one hour when checking for clashes.
Technician
The person doing the work. This drives the clash check and the workload view — and it is what a technician's own calendar is matched on.
Visit Type
Ten types, each with its own colour. See section 5.
Priority
How this ranks against other work on a full day.
Status
Where the visit stands. Start at Scheduled, or Draft if it is only pencilled in.
Additional Attendees
A second technician, an engineer, a client contact — anyone who should be expected on site but is not the assigned technician.
Notes
Scope, site access, gate codes, equipment. The most useful field on the form and the most often left blank.
The clash check

On save, the scheduler looks for another visit for the same technician overlapping the same time, counting only Scheduled, Confirmed and In Progress work. If it finds one it names the technician, the clashing job card and its time.

You can proceed with Override & save anyway — sometimes two jobs on one site genuinely do overlap. The point is that you decided, rather than finding out on the day. Cancelled and completed visits never block a booking.

4 Changing a visit

Open it

Click any block to see its detail — site, time, technician, attendees, type, status, priority and notes. Edit opens the same form you booked it with; Close returns to the calendar.

Drag to move a day

In Month view, drag a visit onto another day. The time of day and the duration are kept exactly; only the date moves.

Dragging sets the status to Rescheduled

A dragged visit is automatically marked Rescheduled — the record should show the appointment moved, not that it was always on the new day. If you are correcting a capture error rather than genuinely moving an appointment, open the visit and set the status back to what it should be.

Dragging does not run the clash check. On a busy week, glance at the technician's other work on the target day before you drop.

Cancelling

Open the visit, choose Edit, then Cancel Visit. The visit stays on the calendar marked Cancelled rather than disappearing, so the history of what was planned survives. Put the reason in Notes before you cancel — a cancelled visit with no explanation is a question somebody has to ask later.

Cancel rather than delete

Deleting removes the record and everything that referenced it. Cancelling keeps the trail. Most roles cannot delete at all; if yours can, treat it as a tool for genuine mistakes — a duplicate created twice by accident — and nothing else.

"Blocks the slot" is the clash check: only Scheduled, Confirmed and In Progress count as the technician being committed. This is why leaving finished work as Scheduled quietly blocks that technician's diary — and why the discipline in section 8 matters.

6 Workload

The Workload screen answers the question the calendar cannot: who has capacity? Use it before you book, not after.

At the top

FigureRead it as
Visits TodayEverything on today's date, whatever its status.
Active NowVisits currently under way — who is on site at this moment.
Hours ScheduledCommitted time across the period. The honest measure of load.
EmergencyEmergency callouts. A rising number here is worth raising.

Below

A per-technician breakdown of visits and hours, and a list of the visits behind the numbers. The same filters as the calendar — technician, status, type and search — narrow all of it together, so you can ask "how much maintenance is Maurice carrying this week?" and get an answer in two clicks.

Use it as a booking check

Before committing a full day to one person, look at their hours here. The calendar shows you that a slot is empty; Workload shows you whether the person is already stretched around it.

7 O&M Tickets

The ticket board is the work itself — the job cards that visits are booked against. Its own navigation sits along the top.

ScreenWhat it holds
DashboardThe state of O&M work at a glance.
Job CardsThe full list, filtered by status.
TriageNew tickets waiting to be assessed and turned into work.
+ New TicketLog a fault or request as it comes in.
+ New Job CardRaise planned work directly, skipping triage.
ClientsClient records and their history.

Ticket statuses

The chips filter the list: All, New, In Progress, Monitoring, Completed, Closed. Monitoring is the useful one — work done, but the site is being watched before anyone calls it closed.

Priority

Normal, High, Urgent. Reserve Urgent for work that displaces other work today. A board where most things are Urgent has no priority at all.

Ticket priority is a label, not a field

+ New Ticket does not create a job card. It writes a note onto an existing one, and records the category and priority inside the note text — [Inverter Fault] [Urgent] Subject…. That means a ticket's priority cannot be filtered, sorted or reported on.

If the work needs a priority the business can act on, raise it with + New Job Card, where Priority is a real field (Critical / High / Medium / Low / Planned Maintenance).

Logging a ticket

  1. Choose + New Ticket.
  2. Pick the Client, or add a new one if they are not listed.
  3. Write a Subject a technician could act on. "Inverter 2 offline since Monday, error E014" beats "inverter problem".
  4. Set the Category, and add the site, asset or inverter, and any error code.
  5. Record who reported it and how to reach them.
  6. Set the Priority and save.

Working a ticket

Open a job card to see its detail, history and notes. Add a note whenever something changes that the next person would need to know — what you found, what you did, what is still outstanding. Notes are timestamped against your name automatically.

Technicians can update Status, Progress %, Site Status and Resource Status. Everything else on a job card is set by Ops or an Admin.

What the job card sheet shows

Alongside client, responsible person, progress and dates, the sheet carries the fields that answer "where is this actually stuck":

A row you cannot see at all is one your role may not read. It is left out rather than shown empty, so an absent row never tells you what is behind it.

The link that makes it all work

Every visit should point at a job card. That link is what lets you open a ticket and see every visit made against it, and what makes the workload figures mean anything. A visit booked without a job card is invisible to all of it.

8 Habits that keep it clean

None of these are enforced by the software. They are what separates a schedule people trust from one they work around.

Close out the day

Finished visits left as Scheduled still block that technician's diary against the clash check, and still count as committed hours in Workload. Five minutes at the end of the day setting them to Completed keeps both honest.

Write the note before you need it

Gate codes, dog on site, which contact actually answers, where the DB board is. The technician standing at a locked gate cannot ask the person who booked it three weeks ago.

Cancel with a reason

Put why in Notes before you cancel. A cancelled visit with no reason turns into a question for somebody later, usually at month end.

Treat an override as a decision

Overriding a clash is sometimes right. Overriding it without looking at what you clashed with is how two technicians end up at the same site while a third is idle.

Book against the job card

If there is no job card, raise one. A visit floating free is work nobody can report on and nobody gets billed for.

Keep technician emails right

A technician on Assigned Only sees a visit only when its technician record carries their exact sign-in address. This is the single most common cause of "my calendar is empty" — see the Handbook.

If something looks wrong

  • Calendar looks empty — check the four filters first.
  • A colleague's booking is missing — wait a minute, or press Refresh.
  • You cannot change a field — it is read-only for your role. Ask Ops.
  • "Could not load the schedule" — press Refresh; if it persists, send the exact wording to an administrator.
